Bitcoin 2026 Las Vegas Recap: Highlights, Announcements, and What's Next

Bitcoin 2026 brought thousands of Bitcoiners to The Venetian Convention and Expo Center in Las Vegas from April 27-29. Attendance was closer to 15,000 than the pre-event projections, but the conference still featured a major speaker lineup, high-profile policy discussions, corporate treasury conversations, and cultural moments across Bitcoin's expanding ecosystem. Here are the highlights and what to watch next.

Day 1: Pro/Industry Day (April 27)

The opening day was designated as Pro/Industry Day and featured the conference's heaviest policy and regulatory programming. Code & Country was one of the conference tracks, focusing on the intersection of Bitcoin, regulation, and government policy. The speaker lineup made a statement about how far Bitcoin's relationship with Washington has shifted.

SEC Chair Paul Atkins Makes History

Paul Atkins became the first sitting SEC Chairman to address a Bitcoin conference. He discussed the SEC's evolving approach to digital assets and crypto regulation, signaling a dramatically different posture than the enforcement-first era under his predecessor. His appearance alone was a milestone, and his remarks on the agency's direction were among the most closely watched moments of the conference.

Senator Cynthia Lummis on the BITCOIN Act

Senator Lummis pushed for the BITCOIN Act, which proposes a strategic U.S. Bitcoin reserve. She also highlighted momentum on the CLARITY Act, designed to resolve SEC/CFTC jurisdictional overlaps and provide long-term market structure for Bitcoin. Lummis indicated that markup could occur in May, framing the legislation as critical for America's financial future.

FBI Director and Acting Attorney General on Bitcoin

FBI Director Kash Patel (joining virtually) and Acting Attorney General Todd Blanche appeared in a session titled "Code is Free Speech: Ending the War on Bitcoin." The discussion positioned Bitcoin development and related code as protected speech and signaled reduced enforcement pressure from the DOJ. This tone represents a dramatic shift from the posture of previous administrations toward Bitcoin developers and users.

Metaplanet Lights Up the Sphere

Japanese Bitcoin treasury company Metaplanet secured placement on the Las Vegas Sphere the day before the conference opened. A massive Bitcoin logo alongside the message "Secure the Future with Bitcoin" greeted arriving attendees. CEO Simon Gerovich took the main stage to discuss the company's aggressive Bitcoin accumulation strategy, funded through zero-interest bond issuances. Metaplanet's treasury stood at over 40,177 BTC at the time of the conference.

Bitcoin Price Action

Bitcoin touched $79,000 on Day 1, its highest level since early February. The rally was supported by strong spot Bitcoin ETF inflows during the weeks leading up to the conference. The price retreated to the $76,700-$77,500 range by Day 2 as broader macro conditions and a coinciding FOMC meeting weighed on momentum.

Day 2: Bitcoin at Scale (April 28)

Michael Saylor Keynote

Strategy founder and Executive Chairman Michael Saylor delivered a solo keynote on the Nakamoto stage. Strategy remains the largest publicly traded corporate Bitcoin holder in the world, with a treasury exceeding 800,000 BTC. Saylor's keynotes are consistently among the most attended sessions at the Bitcoin Conference.

Jack Mallers Takes the Stage

Strike CEO and Twenty One co-founder Jack Mallers closed Day 2 on the Nakamoto stage. Mallers has done more than almost anyone to push Bitcoin into mainstream payments and his appearances consistently draw some of the largest crowds at the conference.

The Institutional vs. Cypherpunk Debate

A sharp debate emerged during the conference. Early Bitcoin investor Simon Dixon publicly called the event "compromised," arguing that marketing ETFs and corporate treasury products reverses Bitcoin's founding promise of individual sovereignty. Others viewed the institutional presence as necessary maturation. The tension reflects a deeper structural shift in Bitcoin ownership and is likely to shape the community conversation for years to come.

Day 3: The Future (April 29)

Jack Dorsey

Twitter and Block Inc. founder Jack Dorsey joined via video alongside filmmaker Eugene Jarecki. Dorsey was one of the most buzzed-about speakers at Bitcoin 2026.

Eric Trump on American Bitcoin

Eric Trump, Co-Founder and Chief Strategy Officer of American Bitcoin Corp (Nasdaq: ABTC), appeared on a panel titled "From American Hashrate to Wall Street: How Bitcoin Benefits the U.S." alongside Calamos Investments CEO John Koudounis. American Bitcoin recently deployed nearly 11,300 new mining rigs at its Drumheller, Alberta site, expanding its fleet to roughly 89,000 ASIC miners.

Afroman at 4:20

In a perfectly on-brand scheduling decision, rapper Afroman took the Nakamoto stage at 4:20 PM. Best known for his 2000 hit "Because I Got High," Afroman has become a free speech figure in recent years. Whale Pass holders also had access to an exclusive fireside chat with the artist.

Bitcoin 2027 Announced: Nashville Returns

The conference closed with the announcement that Bitcoin 2027 will be held July 15-17, 2027 at the Music City Center in Nashville, Tennessee. Key Themes

Washington Came to Bitcoin

The presence of the SEC Chair, FBI Director, Acting Attorney General, and multiple Senators at a Bitcoin conference would have been unthinkable two years ago. Bitcoin 2026 made it clear that U.S. government policy is now being shaped in collaboration with the Bitcoin community rather than in opposition to it.

Corporate Treasuries Are the New Normal

Between Strategy's massive BTC holdings, Metaplanet's growing treasury, and the expanding list of public companies adding Bitcoin to their balance sheets, corporate treasury adoption was a dominant theme. Multiple panels explored how institutions are integrating Bitcoin into their financial strategies.

The Culture War Within Bitcoin

The loudest debate at Bitcoin 2026 was not about price. It was about identity. Some longtime Bitcoiners felt the conference had drifted too far from its cypherpunk roots toward corporate and political interests. Others argued that institutional adoption is the mechanism through which Bitcoin achieves global impact. This tension is likely to shape the community conversation for years to come.

What Comes Next
